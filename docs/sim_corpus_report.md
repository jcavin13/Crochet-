# Sim vs count rule on the corpus

_Generated by `cd app && npm run sim:corpus` (`app/scripts/sim-corpus.ts`). Do not edit by hand._

The app's spring sim run on every corpus pattern with the **fast** tolerances (gtol 0.0001 per round, 0.00001 final — what the browser uses for pieces over 600 stitches), compared with the analytic count rule. Shape classes from `spec/sim_config.json` (tau_flat 0.25, count-rule tol ±10 %); nothing was retuned for this report.

**Headline.** On 16 corpus patterns the sim and the count rule agree on 12 (11 of the 14 the count rule calls flat). 0 did not reach the fast tolerance within the iteration caps ("converged" below is relative to the fast tolerance; at the reference tolerances, review R12 found 5 of 16 hit the caps). Disagreements: popcorn-ridge-mat-8 (count rule flat, sim cup); puff-star-coaster-5 (count rule flat, sim ruffle); rep-from-star-rosette-8 (count rule flat, sim cup); teaching-ruffle-shell-doily (count rule ruffle, sim cup).

How to read it: the two models have different sensitivity. The sim reacts to mismatches of a few percent, the count rule tolerates ±10 %, and the sim has no stuffing, gravity or self-collision. A strongly ruffled short piece can score A ≥ W and be classed a cup (review R1). The 3D view therefore presents its class as a tendency, not a verdict, and marks unconverged or fast solves as approximate. "loose loops": the count rule calls the round flat only because its chain loops may sag or stretch (flatness.md). "closed form": a round closes the shape (amigurumi-style); the unstuffed sim relaxes it like an empty bag.

Timings are from the machine that generated the file and vary with load.
