Swatch photo analysis — accuracy on synthetic photos¶

The analyzer (crolab.swatch.analyze) turns a photo of a swatch lying on the printed ArUco marker sheet into a metrically scaled, fronto-parallel image, measures the swatch and estimates gauge (stitches and rows per 10 cm) from the periodicity of the fabric texture.

Everything here is synthetic. Photos come from crolab.swatch.synth: a procedurally rendered swatch (yarn strokes as shaded tubes, per-stitch jitter, row bowing, alternating faces of turned rows) on the rendered sheet, photographed by a pinhole camera with tilt, lighting gradient, vignetting, blur, noise and JPEG compression. The ground truth (homography, swatch size, stitch and row pitch) is known exactly. No real photograph has been validated yet: these numbers bound the geometry and signal-processing error, not the error on real yarn.

Top left: detected marker corners (all used in a RANSAC homography) and the analysed window. Top right: the rectified window at 8 px/mm with the segmented swatch and its minimum-area box. Bottom left: the swatch rotated to its principal axes, with the experimental lattice (row lines and per-row points at a constant texture phase). Bottom right: the two 1-D spectra the gauge comes from — the stitch spectrum is the 2-D power summed over $k_y$ (insensitive to row shear), the row spectrum is the narrow $k_x \approx 0$ strip. Turned single crochet repeats every two rows (alternating faces), so the row spectrum has a peak at half the row frequency; the stitch-type prior (height/width ≈ 0.95 for sc) selects the one-row period.

Sweep: camera tilt × noise¶

Single crochet with varying pitch, and double crochet; tilt 0–40°, noise σ 2–15 (8-bit), with blur and JPEG growing with the noise level. Each photo is independent (new texture seed, camera azimuth, swatch rotation).

rows = []
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)
configs = []
for tilt in (0, 10, 20, 30, 40):
    for noise in (2, 8, 15):
        configs.append(("sc", tilt, noise))
for tilt in (0, 20, 35):
    configs.append(("dc", tilt, 6))
for k, (st, tilt, noise) in enumerate(configs):
    if st == "sc":
        px = float(rng.uniform(5.0, 7.5)); py = px * float(rng.uniform(0.88, 1.0)); n_st, n_r = int(min(20, 125 // px)), 20
    else:
        px = float(rng.uniform(6.0, 7.2)); py = px * float(rng.uniform(1.8, 2.0)); n_st, n_r = int(min(18, 125 // px)), 9
    sw = SwatchSpec(stitch=st, pitch_x_mm=px, pitch_y_mm=py, n_stitches=n_st, n_rows=n_r, seed=200 + k)
    ph = PhotoSpec(tilt_deg=tilt, tilt_azimuth_deg=float(rng.uniform(0, 360)), swatch_angle_deg=float(rng.uniform(-6, 6)),
                   noise_sigma=noise, blur_px=0.6 + noise / 12, jpeg_quality=int(95 - 1.5 * noise), seed=200 + k)
    img, tr = make_photo(sw, ph, LAYOUTS)
    a = analyze_image(img, stitch=st, hook_mm=5.0, layouts=LAYOUTS, lattice=False).result
    gt = a["gauge_from_texture"]
    H_est, H_true = np.array(a["homography_mm_to_px"]), np.array(tr["H_mm_to_px"])
    x0, y0, x1, y1 = LAYOUTS["A4"].window_mm
    grid = np.array([[x, y] for x in np.linspace(x0, x1, 5) for y in np.linspace(y0, y1, 5)], float)
    back = cv2.perspectiveTransform(cv2.perspectiveTransform(grid[None], H_true), np.linalg.inv(H_est))[0]
    rows.append(dict(stitch=st, tilt=tilt, noise=noise,
                     win_err_mm=float(np.linalg.norm(back - grid, axis=1).max()),
                     reproj_rms_mm=a["markers"]["reproj_rms_mm"],
                     tilt_est=a["camera"]["tilt_deg"],
                     w_err=a["swatch"]["bbox_width_mm"] / tr["bbox_width_mm"] - 1,
                     h_err=a["swatch"]["bbox_height_mm"] / tr["bbox_height_mm"] - 1,
                     px_err=gt["stitch_pitch_mm"] / px - 1, py_err=gt["row_pitch_mm"] / py - 1,
                     conf=gt["confidence"], label=gt["confidence_label"]))
print(f"{len(rows)} photos analysed")

Failure modes¶

Irregular fabric. With stitch jitter of 35 % of the pitch and 2 mm row bowing the texture is no longer periodic enough; the analyzer must say so rather than return a confident number.

What this does not show¶

  • Real yarn: fuzz (mohair), variegated colour, dark yarn texture, real stitch anatomy, edge stitches and turning chains, swatches that curl.
  • Swatch thickness: the top of a 3 mm swatch is above the sheet plane, which magnifies it by about thickness / camera distance (~1 % at 30 cm); --thickness-mm corrects this approximately and is not applied here (synthetic swatches are flat).
  • Lens distortion (phone cameras mostly correct it; not modelled).
  • Lace / thread / mesh swatches with large holes (segmentation closes holes; gauge on open lace is untested).
